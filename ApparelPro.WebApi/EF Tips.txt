2. Advice on Your Primary Key Strategy
Leave it as it is. 
Keep Id as your Primary Key, and keep your current unique index setup.

In database design, 
you have two options here, and your current approach is the best practice for Entity Framework Core:

Option A: Your Current Setup (Surrogate Key + Unique Index) - RECOMMENDED
How it works: 

You use an auto-incrementing integer Id as the Primary Key. 
You enforce business uniqueness by adding a unique index on BuyerCode, Order, TypeCode, and StyleCode.

Why it is better:
Simpler Foreign Keys: 
If another table (like ProductionLog or OrderItems) needs to reference this Style record, 
it only needs to store a single integer (StyleId). 
If you change your primary key, the other table would have to store all 4 columns as a massive composite foreign key.

EF Core Performance: 
EF Core handles single-integer keys much more efficiently during tracking, updates, and relationship mapping.

Option B: Composite Primary Key (Natural Key)
How it works: 
You remove Id entirely and make all 4 fields together act as the primary key.

Why it causes headaches: 
If your business rules change later (e.g., a buyer wants to reuse the same style code for two different delivery types under the same order), 
changing a composite primary key is highly destructive to your database schema.

Your current implementation gives you the best of both worlds: 
clean database relationships via the single Id, and data integrity safety via the unique index constraint.
